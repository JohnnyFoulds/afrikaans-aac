## The right PhD for this person

**AAC** — based on who Johannes is, not just what the fields are.

### Why AAC fits the person

**The "I'll show you" engine works best on real, personal, concrete problems.**
Every meaningful thing in the career — the OCR platform built on a trial licence while procurement stalled, the spam classifier before DataRobot was approved, the AAC app before an SLP was involved — starts from the same place: a real problem that needed solving right now. The RAG PhD starts from a literature gap. That is not the natural working mode. A PhD takes 3–4 years. The work needs to pull you forward, not require pushing.

**The AAC PhD is work only you can do.**
The RAG PhD is a very good PhD that someone else could also do. The AAC PhD requires your father, your mother, your language, your engineering background, your willingness to act without institutional permission, and your ability to ship a working system in a weekend. No researcher in Communication Sciences is in this position. No grant-funded study would start this way.

**The builder-who-documents pattern fits DSR perfectly.**
Best work is always: build the thing, measure it rigorously, extract the principles. The AAC PhD is exactly that. The artefact exists. The first DSR cycle is live. The principles are emergent.

**The stakes are personal.**
You will not feel the weight of the RAG PhD at 11pm debugging a phrase that isn't playing. You will feel the AAC PhD every time you visit your parents.

### The one honest counterargument

The RAG PhD is closer to the professional identity as an AI engineer. It is easier to explain to a hiring manager. The AAC PhD will confuse people who expect the PhD to be in ML or NLP.

That matters if the PhD is for career advancement. It matters less if the PhD is for the work itself.
