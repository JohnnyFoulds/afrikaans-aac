# Vodafone Group and AAC: Web Research Report

**Date of research:** 2026-04-05
**Researcher:** Claude (web research via agent)
**Scope:** Vodafone Group plc (vodafone.com), Vodafone Foundation (vodafonefoundation.org), Vodafone UK (vodafone.co.uk), national foundations (Spain, Italy, Portugal, Germany), academic literature, and Vodafone–Vodacom group-level disability/accessibility policy chain.

---

## Executive Summary

**Vodafone Group has no activity specifically in AAC (Augmentative and Alternative Communication), aphasia, or post-stroke communication rehabilitation.** Its disability-adjacent work is substantial but focused on: (a) Deaf/hearing-impaired relay services (Relay UK); (b) smart accessibility app developer competitions (Smart Accessibility Awards, 2011–2012); (c) IoT and connected living for people with learning disabilities (Connected Living / Mencap, 2019–2020); (d) neurodiversity and workplace inclusion research; and (e) general visual/motor disability technology showcases using 5G. The closest direct AAC-adjacent find is the 2012 Smart Accessibility Award winner Ablah, an autism AAC app built by a third-party developer with Foundation prize money. This is not a Vodafone-developed or Vodafone-operated AAC tool.

No partnership exists between Vodafone Group and Tobii Dynavox, AssistiveWare, Smartbox, Therapy Box, RCSLT, the Aphasia Alliance, or any AAC organisation. No peer-reviewed academic paper links Vodafone Group to AAC or speech-language therapy. The Vodafone–Vodacom group policy chain (ESG, "Spirit" / "Doing What's Right") sets general human rights and inclusion frameworks but contains no disability-specific mandates that directly translate to AAC provision at the Vodacom SA subsidiary level.

## Section 1: Vodafone Foundation — Smart Accessibility Awards (2011–2012)

### 1.1 Programme Overview

- **Name:** Vodafone Foundation Smart Accessibility Awards
- **Timeframe:** Launched June 2011; awards ceremony December 2012 (second cycle); discontinued after 2012
- **Geographic scope:** European Union
- **Partners:** AGE Platform Europe; European Disability Forum (EDF)
- **Prize fund:** €200,000 split across four categories
- **Categories:** (1) Social participation; (2) Independent living; (3) Wellbeing; (4) Mobility
- **Platform requirement:** Android 2.2 or above
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/digital-society/smart-accessibility-awards

### 1.2 AAC-Relevant Winner: Ablah (2012 — Wellbeing Category)

- **Developer:** Juan Carlos Gonzalez Montesino and Manuel Linares (Spain)
- **Motivation:** Montesino built the app to help his five-year-old autistic son Darío communicate
- **Description:** Ablah is an Android app for augmentative communication via image/symbol–sound–text exchange on touchscreen devices. The user taps an image and the app produces an automated voice output. This is a PECS-based (Picture Exchange Communication System) AAC app.
- **Prize:** €50,000 (Wellbeing category winner)
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/empowering-people/smartaccess-winners
- **AAC relevance:** HIGH. This is a direct AAC app — image-based symbol communication with synthesized voice output — funded by a Vodafone Foundation prize. However, Vodafone did not develop, distribute, or operate this app; it funded the developer competition from which this emerged. The Ablah app was a third-party project; there is no evidence of continued Vodafone involvement after the prize was awarded.

### 1.3 Other 2012 Winners (not AAC-specific)

- **Jaccede** (Mobility category): Map of step-free/wheelchair-accessible places
- **Happen** (Independent living category): Information access for elderly and visually impaired users
- **Starting Blocks** (Social participation): Android skills training for elderly users new to smartphones

### 1.4 Programme Status

The Smart Accessibility Awards ran for at least two cycles (2011, 2012). The developer portal (developer.vodafone.com/smartaccess2012) is no longer accessible. There is no evidence of a third cycle or continuation of the programme. The Vodafone Foundation has since refocused its three pillars on digital learning, tackling harm and abuse, and crisis response — none of which includes disability technology or AAC.

## Section 2: Vodafone Business Ventures / Mencap — Connected Living (2019–2020)

### 2.1 Programme Overview

- **Name:** Connected Living
- **Timeframe:** 12-month pilot (2018–2019); announced publicly July 2019; formal launch January 2020
- **Partners:** Vodafone Business Ventures (Vodafone's social enterprise arm) + Mencap (UK learning disability charity)
- **Geographic scope:** UK (30 Mencap supported-living care homes)
- **Technology:** IoT-enabled devices — smart plugs, activity sensors, smart door locks — connected via Vodafone's MyLife app. Suite of personalised digital services.
- **Features:** (a) My Day: diary management with daily reminders; (b) to-do lists with visual guides for everyday life skills; (c) call button to support worker; (d) Oyster Watch: GPS wrist-watch connected to support worker; (e) **My Talk**: image-tap-to-speech AAC communication app
- **Source URL (1):** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/digital-society/connected-living-scheme-mencap-disability
- **Source URL (2):** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/empowering-people/connected-living-how-vodafone-and-mencap-are-supporting-people-learning-disabilities

### 2.2 My Talk — The AAC-Adjacent Component

Within the Connected Living suite, a resident named Matt Massey (described as "verbally challenged") uses the **My Talk** app. The press article states: "Matt Massey, a verbally challenged Mencap resident, uses the 'My Talk' app on his tablet to help with communication. When he taps on an image, an automated voice speaks for him. 'I am happy,' it says."

- **AAC relevance:** HIGH. The My Talk app, as described, is an image-to-voice AAC application — the same functional design as commercial AAC apps like Proloquo2Go or Snap Core First. It was developed as part of the Vodafone × Mencap Connected Living project.
- **Critical caveats:**
  1. The target population is people with **learning disabilities** (intellectual disability), not acquired communication disorders such as post-stroke aphasia.
  2. There is no evidence the My Talk app was commercialised, released publicly, or is still active.
  3. This was a pilot involving only 30 care homes in the UK.
  4. The Vodafone newsroom has not published any update on Connected Living after January 2020; the programme appears not to have been scaled.

## Section 3: Vodafone UK — Accessibility Services for Communication-Impaired Customers

### 3.1 Relay UK Participation

- **Service:** Relay UK (BT-operated; all UK mobile operators legally required under Ofcom General Conditions C5.8 to provide relay access)
- **Vodafone participation:** Confirmed (listed on vodafone.co.uk/help/accessibility: "If you're deaf, hard of hearing or speech impaired you can: Make calls with Relay UK")
- **Service types offered:** Text relay (Relay UK), 999 BSL (emergency sign language), SignVideo for BSL (video relay), registration for text emergency services
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.co.uk/help/accessibility
- **AAC relevance:** Low–moderate. Relay UK enables text-to-voice and voice-to-text relayed calls, which a speech-impaired aphasia user who can type could theoretically use. However, this is a generic telephony relay service with no AAC-specific vocabulary or symbol support.

### 3.2 Hidden Disabilities Sunflower Programme (2022)

- **Description:** Vodafone became the first technology communications company to sign up to the Hidden Disabilities Sunflower scheme — a symbol allowing people with hidden disabilities (including speech impairments) to signal to retail staff that they need extra assistance.
- **Geographic scope:** UK retail stores + employee workplace
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/empowering-people/not-every-disability-visible-hybrid-world
- **AAC relevance:** Indirect. A customer who uses AAC might benefit from retail staff being more patient and accommodating, but this is a customer service programme, not an AAC initiative.

### 3.3 WCAG 2.1 Website Accessibility

- **Statement:** "Our goal is for everyone to be able to use our website. We aim to meet AA standards of the World Wide Web Consortium's Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1"
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.co.uk/help/accessibility
- **AAC relevance:** None specific to AAC.

### 3.4 GARI (Global Accessibility Reporting Initiative)

- **Statement:** Vodafone supports GARI, which helps customers find devices with the accessibility features they need (screen readers, hearing aid compatibility, etc.)
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.co.uk/help/accessibility
- **AAC relevance:** Indirect. GARI reports device features including speech output and text-to-speech which are AAC-adjacent, but no Vodafone-specific AAC device listing was found.

## Section 5: Vodafone Group — Disability and Neurodiversity (Workplace/Internal)

### 5.1 Remote4All Research with Coventry University (2018–2022)

- **Partners:** Vodafone + Coventry University (Centre for Healthcare Research), NHS Employers, Coventry City Council, Leonard Cheshire, Dyslexia Box, SEND
- **Funded by:** UK Economic and Social Research Council (UKRI Digital Futures at Work Research Centre Innovation Fund)
- **Geographic scope:** UK, Ireland, Czech Republic, Turkey
- **Description:** Research into lived experiences of remote working for people with disabilities and neurodiverse conditions. - **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/empowering-people/vodafone-coventry-university-disabilities-neurodiverse-conditions

- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/news/newsroom/empowering-people/neurodiversity-in-the-tech-sector

## Section 8: Vodafone–Vodacom Group Policy Chain — Does Group Disability Policy Reach South Africa?

### 8.1 Group ESG Framework ("Empowering People")

Vodafone Group's ESG strategy has three pillars: (1) Empowering people; (2) Protecting the planet; (3) Maintaining trust. Under "Empowering People," the key subcomponents are:

- Closing the digital divide (coverage, affordability, devices, digital skills)
- Empowering customers (SME digitalisation, financial inclusion, M-PESA)
- Supporting communities (diversity and inclusion, Foundation programmes)

Disability is addressed only under "closing the digital divide" (device accessibility, relay services for country-level regulatory compliance) and "supporting communities" (general D&I workforce inclusion). There is no group-level disability programme specifically mandating AAC provision, speech-language therapy, or communication rehabilitation at any subsidiary.

- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/sustainable-business/empowering-people

### 8.2 "Doing What's Right" Code of Conduct (formerly "Spirit")

Vodafone's Code of Conduct (formerly branded "Spirit of Vodafone") governs employee behaviour and ethical standards across all markets including Vodacom. The code includes anti-discrimination provisions and references to human rights (UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights). It does not mandate specific disability technology provision, AAC adoption, or communication support programmes.

- **Disability in the code:** General non-discrimination and accommodation principles; no specific disability category programme mandates.
- **Source URL:** https://www.vodafone.com/sustainable-business/maintaining-trust/society/human-rights

### 8.3 Vodacom Group Relationship Structure

Vodafone Group plc holds approximately 60.5% of Vodacom Group Ltd (JSE-listed). Vodacom South Africa is a 65% subsidiary of Vodacom Group. The governance structure means:

- Group ESG targets (e.g., 40% women in management by 2030) apply to Vodacom
- Group policy frameworks (human rights, responsible purchasing, privacy) apply to Vodacom
- Group-funded initiatives (Vodafone Foundation) do **not** automatically extend to Vodacom — the Vodacom Foundation is a separate entity with its own funding and priorities

**Conclusion:** Vodafone Group's accessibility/disability framework sets a floor of compliance (relay services per national regulation, WCAG website standards, non-discrimination) but does not drive AAC-specific initiatives downstream to Vodacom SA. The Vodafone Foundation's Connected Living (Mencap/UK) programme has no counterpart in any Vodacom African market. The Smart Accessibility Awards (EU only, 2011–2012) was never extended to African markets.

### 8.4 Vodafone Foundation vs Vodacom Foundation — Separate Entities

The Vodafone Foundation (UK charity, reg. 1193984) and the Vodacom Foundation (South African NPO) are legally separate entities with separate governance, funding, and programme choices. The Vodafone Foundation's three current pillars (digital learning, harm/abuse, crisis response) differ from the Vodacom Foundation's focus (education, gender, GBV prevention in South Africa). Neither foundation has AAC or speech-language therapy in its programme portfolio.

## Strategic Observations for the PhD

1. **No specific AAC activity at Vodafone Group level.** The null finding is unambiguous: Vodafone does not partner with AAC organisations, does not manufacture or distribute AAC tools, and does not fund AAC research or clinical programmes.

2. **The Ablah precedent is real but weak.** The 2012 Smart Accessibility Awards produced a genuine AAC app (symbol-based, touch-activated speech output) via a third-party developer. This demonstrates that the Vodafone Foundation has previously been willing to prize-fund AAC-type applications, but the programme was discontinued, the app was not Vodafone-developed, and there has been no revival.

3. **The My Talk / Connected Living precedent is real but dated and scoped.** Vodafone Business Ventures developed an IoT-connected AAC communication feature (image-tap-to-speech) for people with learning disabilities in Mencap care homes (UK, 2019). This is the closest Vodafone has come to developing an in-house AAC tool. The programme was a pilot only, was not commercialised, and the target population (adults with intellectual/learning disabilities in supported living) differs from post-stroke aphasia (acquired communication disorder in a previously neurotypical adult).

4. **No bridge point between Vodacom and the AAC industry was found.** The apparent "Tobii" reference at the 2024 Accessibility Conference was Vodacom's own chatbot "Tobi" — unrelated to Tobii Dynavox. There is no documented intersection between the Vodacom/Vodafone ecosystem and the AAC industry in any source searched.

5. **Group policy chain does not deliver AAC to South Africa.** Vodafone Group's disability framework (Doing What's Right, human rights, ESG targets) creates a non-discrimination and inclusion baseline but does not mandate or fund specific assistive communication provision. Vodacom SA's disability programme (Specific Needs) was built locally and reflects the South African regulatory context (Ofcom equivalent: ICASA), not a Vodafone Group programme rollout.

6. **The gap is structurally consistent.** Both Vodafone Group (globally) and Vodacom SA (locally) have classified communication disability primarily through the Deaf/hearing-impaired lens (relay services, SASL, sign language). Speech impairment from stroke or acquired neurological conditions falls into a different clinical and regulatory category — one that neither telco has operationalised.

7. **Potential future hook — Vodafone Foundation pivot.** The Vodafone Foundation's stated commitment to "making digital skills and learning available, accessible and inclusive for those who are most underserved" could theoretically extend to post-stroke aphasia (acquired illiteracy/communication loss) as an underserved population. The #Unplugged and HiDigital programmes work with older adults across Europe; a strand addressing acquired communication disorders in older adults is a logical extension that does not currently exist.
