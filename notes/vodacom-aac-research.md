# Vodacom and AAC: Web Research Report

**Date of research:** 2026-04-05
**Researcher:** Claude (web research via agent)
**Scope:** Vodacom South Africa (vodacom.co.za), Vodacom Foundation, MTN SA (comparator), academic literature

---

## Executive Summary

**Vodacom has no activity in AAC (Augmentative and Alternative Communication), aphasia, or post-stroke communication rehabilitation.** Its disability work is entirely in adjacent areas: Deaf/hearing-impaired relay services, visual impairment devices, and internal HR inclusion. The same null finding applies to MTN South Africa. No peer-reviewed academic paper exists linking any South African telco to AAC or speech-language therapy.

---

## Section 1: Vodacom Products and Services — Disability and Communication

### 1.1 Specific Needs Programme (since 2004)

- **Live since:** 2004 (original programme); expanded continuously
- **Description:** Vodacom's oldest disability initiative. Provides disability-registered customers with: priority queue in stores and for repairs, special contract deals (data/SMS only, no voice — for Deaf/speech-impaired customers), accessible devices, alternative billing formats (Braille, large print, audio), and a dedicated contact centre (12580).
- **Scope:** Three disability categories: (1) persons with visual impairments; (2) persons with communication barriers (Deaf, hard of hearing, speech impaired, DeafBlind); (3) senior citizens.
- **Source:** https://www.vodacom.co.za/vodacom/shopping/v/specific-needs
- **AAC relevance:** The "speech impaired" category under "persons with communication barriers" is the closest to AAC users, but the support offered is customer service infrastructure (relay calls, SMS bundles, priority repairs) rather than communication aids or AAC devices. A post-stroke aphasia user who is not Deaf and who can use a touchscreen would fall outside the primary target population of this programme.

### 1.2 BlindShell Classic 2 Phone (launched 1 July 2025)

- **Description:** A physical-button smartphone with voice control, tactile keypad, loud speaker, and a curated app catalogue. Designed for blind and partially sighted users. Available on prepaid and contract.
- **Source:** https://www.vodacom.com/news-article.php?articleID=15834
- **AAC relevance:** None. Visual impairment assistive device only.

### 1.3 Vodacom 112 Emergency App (since 2017, with ER24)

- **Description:** Allows Deaf, hearing-impaired, and speech-impaired customers to contact emergency services (police, ambulance, fire, sea rescue) via SMS/text rather than voice calls. App available from app stores; zero-rated via ConnectU.
- **Source:** https://it-online.co.za/2025/09/12/er24-and-vodacom-reach-out-to-sas-deaf-community/
- **AAC relevance:** Tangentially relevant: speech-impaired users (which could include aphasia users) can access emergency services. However, this is emergency-only infrastructure, not an AAC communication tool.

## Section 3: Partnerships

### 3.1 ER24 (Emergency Medical Services)

- **Since:** 2017 (112 Emergency App), expanded to NRS management
- **Description:** ER24 operates Vodacom's National Relay Service (NRS) contact centre. Eight national relay officers (3 qualified SASL interpreters, 5 handling voice queries for blind/paraplegic patients). Deaf and speech-impaired users can reach ER24 via video, voice, or text relay.
- **Source:** https://it-online.co.za/2025/09/12/er24-and-vodacom-reach-out-to-sas-deaf-community/
- **AAC relevance:** Low. The NRS relay infrastructure is designed for Deaf/hearing-impaired users (SASL), not for people with acquired aphasia who can read but cannot speak.

### 3.2 National Institute for the Deaf (NID) (since January 2026)

- **Description:** Vodacom became the first South African organisation to embed South African Sign Language (SASL) into the workplace. A 12-module SASL course developed with NID; SASL font integrated into Microsoft 365; SASL dictionary added to the Employee Engage app.
- **Source:** https://now.vodacom.co.za/brand-with-a-purpose/vodacom-shows-the-way-in-creating-equal-opportunities-for-persons-with-disabilities/
- **AAC relevance:** None — internal HR/workplace initiative for Deaf employees, not a customer-facing AAC programme.

### 3.3 SASLHA, Aphasia SA, Universities

- **Finding:** No partnership with SASLHA (South African Speech-Language-Hearing Association), Aphasia SA, any South African university speech-language pathology department, or any AAC organisation was found in any searched source.

## Section 4: Connectivity / Data Access for Disability

### 4.1 National Relay Service (NRS) (launched 18 November 2022)

- **Status:** First relay service launched by a mobile network operator in South Africa (Vodacom's own claim).
- **Description:** Free, real-time relay service for registered Vodacom prepaid and postpaid customers who are Deaf, hearing-impaired, or speech-impaired. Five service modes:
  1. Voice relay (call 082 12580) — user can speak but struggles to hear
  2. Text relay (SMS 12580) — user cannot hear or speak
  3. Live chat relay (WhatsApp / My Vodacom App) — text-based
  4. Video relay — SASL users connect to a SASL interpreter who voices the message to the called party
  5. Captioned telephony — speech-to-text on devices with Live Captions
- **Source:** https://www.vodacom.com/news-article.php?articleID=7853
- **AAC relevance:** Low–moderate. The text relay and live chat relay modes could theoretically be used by a post-stroke aphasia user who can type but not speak. However, the service is designed for Deaf/hearing-impaired users, does not integrate with AAC devices or software, and provides no AAC-specific vocabulary or symbol support.

### 4.2 ConnectU Zero-Rated Platform

- **Description:** Zero-rated access to job sites, Facebook, basic news, and emergency services information. Emergency services for communication-barriers customers are explicitly zero-rated.
- **Source:** https://www.vodacom.co.za/vodacom/services/connectu
- **AAC relevance:** None directly. No AAC apps or speech therapy tools are zero-rated. Clinics and hospitals can apply for zero-rated status via ConnectU — this is a possible future avenue for an AAC PWA, but no existing programme exists.

### 4.3 Special SMS Bundles for Communication Barriers Customers

- **Description:** Double-value SMS bundles at reduced per-SMS rates. Targeted at Deaf and hearing-impaired customers who prefer SMS over voice.
- **Source:** https://www.vodacom.co.za/vodacom/services/specific-needs-persons-with-communication-barriers
- **AAC relevance:** None for AAC users.

## Strategic Observations for the PhD

1. **No competitive ecosystem to position against** — the post-stroke/aphasia/AAC space is entirely unoccupied by any South African telco.

2. **The NRS text/chat relay is a theoretically usable channel** for a typing-capable aphasia user, but Vodacom has not designed for or marketed to this population.

3. **ConnectU zero-rating precedent** — clinics and hospitals can apply for zero-rated access to health services; a future avenue would be applying to zero-rate an AAC progressive web app, but there is no existing programme to tap into.

4. **The SAMRC presence at the 2025 Disability Conference** suggests Vodacom may be open to public health partnerships — speculative but worth monitoring.

5. **The gap in academic literature is itself a finding** — no researcher has studied telco involvement in AAC in South Africa. This absence contextualises both the novelty of this project and the lack of infrastructure support for AAC device users.
