## Approach Strategy

### Step 1 — Framing the PhD as aligned work, not parallel work

The conversation to have is not "may I do a PhD alongside my job." It is:

> *"I've been building an Afrikaans AAC tool for my father-in-law for the past year. It's live and he uses it every day. The research framing I'm developing around it addresses a gap in African language AI that I think is directly relevant to Vodacom's product strategy. I want to talk about what a Vodacom-facilitated PhD would look like — specifically whether there's a way to structure it so the research benefits both the academic contribution and Vodacom."*

This frames it as a strategic asset, not a personal project Schalk is being asked to accommodate.

### Step 2 — Use the literature review document

The [phd-ai-framing-review.md](../../literature/reviews/phd-ai-framing-review.md) is the right artifact to share. It is evidence-based, technically grounded, and maps the four-way research gap clearly. It speaks Schalk's language: AI credibility, applied rigour, institutional leverage. It is also the document that demonstrates the PhD is not a vague idea but a thought-through research programme.

Do not pitch from memory — share the document and let him engage with it.

### Step 3 — The ask

There are three things Vodacom could provide:
1. **Time allocation** — research time treated as part of role, not an add-on
2. **Data access** — Vodacom usage data, network data, or internal NLP resources relevant to African language AI
3. **Co-authorship / acknowledgement** — legitimate credit for Vodacom's contribution to published research

The ask does not have to include all three at once. Starting with time allocation (even 20%) is the most pragmatic entry point.
