## Approach Strategy

### Step 1 — Start with a conversation, not a pitch

Because the relationship is already "sort-of-friends," the opening does not need to be formal. A direct message or informal conversation is appropriate:

> *"I've been working on something for a while that I think sits right at the intersection of what you do — African language AI deployed on commodity hardware in SA. I'd love to get your take on it. Do you have 30 minutes sometime?"*

This is a genuine request for his perspective, not a sponsorship ask. His response to the work will tell you whether formal involvement makes sense.

### Step 2 — Lead with the technical problem, not the human story

Jaco's hook is not the clinical story (aphasia, stroke, father-in-law) — that is Karen's hook. Lead with the research problem:

> *"There is no adult Afrikaans clinical vocabulary corpus. No NLP model has been evaluated on adult conversational Afrikaans. The best Afrikaans ASR result on preschool narrative data is 33.7% WER after fine-tuning with 30 minutes of in-domain audio. I'm building the first AI-assisted AAC system for Afrikaans post-stroke aphasia, deployed on a commodity Android tablet, fully offline. The research questions are about what happens when you push a multilingual model into a low-resource language domain with almost no data and no connectivity."*

Then let him engage with the technical constraints. He will have opinions — listen to them.

### Step 3 — Raise the supervision question naturally

If the conversation goes well, the question raises itself:

> *"You're already supervising students at AIMS and Stellenbosch. I'm trying to figure out what the right institutional home for this research would be. Would you be interested in being involved in some capacity — even informally — as a Vodacom research partner?"*

The "even informally" framing lowers the commitment barrier and lets him define what involvement looks like. He may have existing channels with Stellenbosch supervisors who would be the right fit.

### What to avoid

- Do not pitch the human story first — he will engage with it eventually, but the technical credibility needs to be established first
- Do not be vague about the methodology — he will ask; have specific answers ready
- Do not overstate the current state of the AI — be honest that Features A and B are planned, not built; the corpus does not exist yet; the PhD is the plan to build it
- Do not ask for funding — that is not his role; his value is technical credibility, academic connections, and co-supervision
