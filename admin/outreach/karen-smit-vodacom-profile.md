## Her Motivating Frames (use her own language)

These are the phrases she uses consistently — mirror them:

| Her language | Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Intentional inclusion" | Proactive design, not reactive afterthought |
| "Nothing about us without us" | Co-design with disabled people is non-negotiable |
| "Equitable customer experiences" | Disabled people as consumers and citizens, not charity recipients |
| "Leave no one behind" | Systemic change, not individual gestures |
| "Digital participation = economic empowerment" | AT as economic infrastructure, not welfare |

She explicitly rejects the "charity" framing of disability. She advocates for disabled people as **consumers** with rights and purchasing power, not as recipients of goodwill.

## Approach Strategy

### Step 1 — Warm introduction via wife (not a cold pitch)

Karen is relationship-first. The personal connection is the unlock. Wife reaches out first:

> *"I know you and what you stand for. My husband has built something I genuinely think will interest you. Can we find 20 minutes?"*

This almost certainly gets a yes.

### Step 2 — First meeting: conversation, not pitch

Let her ask questions. Listen to her frames. She will tell you what she wants to champion. Do not lead with funding ask.

### Step 3 — Lead with the human story

> *"My father-in-law had a stroke and lost his speech. He can read, understands everything, still has things to say — but he can't say them. So we built him a tool. It's running on his tablet today, in Afrikaans. We're turning that into research that could help every stroke survivor in Africa facing the same wall."*

Then connect to her world:

> *"Acquired communication disabilities after stroke are massively underserved in African AT. There is no Afrikaans AAC on the market. We're documenting the design process so it can be replicated — and we're looking for institutional partners who understand that assistive technology is not charity, it's infrastructure."*

### What to avoid

- Do not lead with "PhD research" — lead with the person and the tool
- Do not frame it as charity
- Do not pitch Vodacom funding in the first conversation — let her identify the opportunity
- Do not use generic disability language — use her vocabulary
