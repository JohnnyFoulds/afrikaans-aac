# PhD Assessment: AAC vs UNISA RAG — Honest Personalised Comparison

## Context

Johannes is a Senior AI Engineer at Vodacom (MSc Data Science & AI, Distinction, 2025) considering two parallel PhD tracks. This is not an abstract academic exercise — both have real application infrastructure underway. The question is which to prioritise and ultimately pursue. This assessment uses an evidence-based rubric derived from the doctoral completion literature (Sverdlik et al. 2018, CGS Completion Project, Nature PhD surveys) applied honestly and specifically to Johannes's situation.

---

## The Rubric: 10 Dimensions in 3 Tiers

### Tier 1 — Completion Determinants (near-veto conditions; a score of 1–2 here means HIGH RISK regardless of everything else)

| # | Dimension | Weight | Evidence basis |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------|
| D1 | Supervisor quality and fit | 20% | Consistently the #1 predictor of completion and satisfaction in every major longitudinal study |
| D3 | Scope feasibility and completion risk | 15% | Over-ambition is the leading cause of non-completion; "ordinary originality, well executed" is the target |
| D5 | Funding and institutional security | 10% | Financial insecurity and institutional risk are major documented stressors and attrition predictors |

### Tier 2 — Persistence and Satisfaction Drivers (predict whether you thrive, not just survive)

| # | Dimension | Weight | Evidence basis |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------|
| D2 | Intrinsic motivation and topic passion | 15% | Autonomous motivation predicts persistence through adversity; controlled motivation (credential only) predicts lower wellbeing and higher dropout risk |
| D6 | Departmental environment and research community | 10% | Departmental socialisation is the second most consistent completion predictor after supervision |
| D9 | Personal resilience fit and life context | 10% | Part-time, full-time-employed, geographically distributed — these compound PhD difficulty significantly |
| D10 | Topic–person capability match | 7% | "Academic identity" formation predicts completion; skill-readiness at entry matters |

### Tier 3 — Value Maximisers (shape what the PhD is worth, not whether it is completed)

| # | Dimension | Weight | Evidence basis |
|---|-----------|--------|----------------|
| D7 | Career pathway alignment | 8% | Matters for long-term motivation maintenance; alumni placement in desired roles |
| D4 | Originality and academic defensibility | 3% | Necessary condition but not sufficient; "ordinary originality" beats ambitious but incomplete |
| D8 | Publication potential and impact | 2% | More important for academic careers; less critical for industry-track PhDs |

## The Honest Reading of the Numbers

The weighted scores are nearly identical: 3.57 vs 3.47. This is not a toss-up — it reflects a genuine trade-off between two different risk profiles.

**The RAG PhD scores higher on the structural/operational dimensions.** Better supervisor accessibility, cleaner scope, lower implementation risk, better life-context fit, higher capability match. It is the more reliably completable PhD.

**The AAC PhD scores higher on the meaning/impact dimensions.** Far higher intrinsic motivation, far higher originality, better departmental environment if Stellenbosch works out, higher publication impact. It is the more worthwhile PhD.

The near-identical aggregate score obscures the structure of the difference. These are not equivalent options — they are options with opposite failure modes:

| Risk | UNISA RAG PhD | AAC PhD |
|------|--------------|---------|
| Primary failure mode | Complete it, but run out of motivation in year 2 when GPT-5 makes BM25 look quaint | Fail to secure a supervisor, or the MSc eligibility blocks Stellenbosch |
| Secondary failure mode | Credential without meaningful contribution | Scope creep on ASR component; participant health risk |
| Upside case | Clean, well-executed PhD completed in minimum time; two solid papers | Landmark paper in low-resource AT; first adult Afrikaans vocabulary corpus; career-defining work |

## The Personalised Verdict

**The AAC PhD is the right PhD for who Johannes is. The UNISA RAG PhD is the safe PhD.**

This is not a close call on values, even if it is a close call on numbers.

Here is why, stated directly:

**1. The motivation literature is not speculative.** Johannes is doing this part-time, employed full-time, self-funded, without a cohort. The nights when the PhD is hard — debugging an Afrikaans ASR model at 11pm, rewriting chapter 3 for the third time, fighting with the institutional ethics review board — those nights happen to everyone. The difference between people who finish and people who don't is usually not ability. It is whether there is a reason to sit back down the next morning. The RAG PhD's reason is "credential." The AAC PhD's reason is "my father cannot speak." These are not equivalent motivations.

**2. Johannes already knows this.** He wrote: "You will not feel the weight of the RAG PhD at 11pm debugging a phrase that isn't playing. You will feel the AAC PhD every time you visit your parents." He has already done the analysis. The decision file is titled "Decision reached — action plan defined" and its verdict is: "The AAC PhD is the right PhD."

**3. The risks are different in kind, not just degree.** The RAG PhD's primary risk is a slow, grinding completion — a PhD that drains rather than energises, producing work that is overtaken by commercial LLMs before the ink is dry on the certificate. This is a completion risk of a particular kind: not the clean failure of a blocked application, but the long failure of diminishing returns and eroding motivation. The AAC PhD's risks are cleaner and more actionable: supervisor access, MSc eligibility, clinical co-supervisor. These are problems that can be investigated, resolved, or worked around. The Niesler group's existence is a fact. Jaco's willingness to make an introduction can be tested with one conversation.

**4. Only Johannes can do the AAC PhD.** Anyone with a corpus and a GPU can do the RAG PhD. The AAC PhD requires his father, his mother, his language, his engineering background, his ability to ship in a weekend, and his willingness to act before asking permission. No grant-funded researcher is in this position. This is an argument about uniqueness and about the kind of contribution that only this person, in this moment, can make.

**5. The credential argument for the RAG PhD is less compelling than it appears.** Johannes is already a Senior AI Engineer. He is not doing a PhD to change jobs — he is doing it alongside a job. The "harder to explain to a hiring manager" concern about the AAC PhD is real but manageable, especially within Vodacom's African language AI narrative. And in 10 years, "I built the first adult Afrikaans AAC system for my father and published the first adult Afrikaans clinical vocabulary corpus" is a better story than "I compared BM25 to dense retrieval on UNISA tutorial letters."
